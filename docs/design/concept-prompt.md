# The prompt the concept sheets are drawn from

Paste the whole block into an image model (it draws one sheet per reply; write `next` for the following one).
Sheets 1–5 exist in `docs/design/concepts/`; 6–10 are still to be drawn.

```text
You are the concept artist for an original game, "Chronicle of Winds". Below is the world bible and a list of 10 concept sheets.

HOW TO WORK. Generate ONE sheet per reply, as one image, starting with SHEET 1. After each image, stop and wait. When I write "next", generate the following sheet. If I ask for a change, redo the same sheet. Keep the world, style and palette below fixed in every image, and keep every character's design identical across sheets.

WORLD. A thousand years ago the land drowned in fog. People lifted islands into the sky on glowing crystals of "etherite" and now live above an endless sea of clouds. Islands are inverted cones of rock with a flat grassy top; pale-cyan etherite crystals grow from their undersides and glow. Ships do not use balloons or propellers: each ship carries one large etherite crystal in a brass cage under the keel, and sails made of "wind silk". The capital, Spark Harbour, is a small trading town around the Wind Exchange — a round stone-and-plaster windmill tower with four canvas blades, where a glowing brass-framed board shows prices. It is the age of sail (17th–18th century feel), cosy and a little shabby, never grim, no weapons drawn, no skulls.

STYLE. Hand-drawn concept sheet: confident pen-and-ink line with cross-hatching for shadow, over flat watercolour washes. Looks like an antique illustrated travel journal. Chunky, readable silhouettes, slightly exaggerated proportions (large hands, long coats, big hats), everything patched, mended and lived-in. Not photorealistic, not anime, not a glossy 3D render. Do not imitate any specific existing book, film, game or artist; this is an original world.

PALETTE (use only these, plus skin tones): sky top #3d6ea8, warm horizon #f2c58a, cloud sea #f4ece4 with shadow #a9b4cf, grass #7fb069, cliff rock #b07a57, etherite glow #7fe3ff, timber #8a5a3c, dark timber #5e3b28, plaster #efe2c8, roof red #c4643f, roof teal #3f8f8a, sail canvas #f3e9d2, brass #d9a441, sash red #a8322a, navy #22324f. Late-afternoon light: warm sun from the upper left, cool blue shadows.

PURPOSE. These sheets will be rebuilt as simple 3D models from boxes, cylinders and cones with flat colours. So: clear shapes over fine detail, every part separable and nameable, no lettering or logos on clothes or signs (use pictograms: an anchor, a quill, a coin, a sail).

SHEET RULES. Landscape 16:9, plain off-white paper background, neutral standing pose for people, orthographic views (no perspective distortion), the same design in every view, short hand-written English labels with thin leader lines, a row of colour swatches with hex codes at the bottom.

SHEET 6 — HOUSES KIT. A modular kit for half-timbered harbour houses, drawn as separate parts in orthographic front view, then two assembled houses (one storey and two storeys) in three-quarter view. Parts: stone plinth, timber-frame wall panel with diagonal braces, window with shutters and flower box, plank door with stone steps, steep clay-tile gable roof with deep eaves, crooked chimney, bracket lantern, hanging sign with a pictogram, a jettied upper floor, an outside stair, a small balcony with drying laundry. Houses lean slightly, each a little different.

SHEET 7 — THE WIND EXCHANGE. Exterior: front elevation and a three-quarter view of a round windmill tower on a stone base with a stepped terrace, a gallery with a railing halfway up, four large canvas blades with lattice, a teal conical roof with a brass finial, a double door under a brass lintel. Interior cutaway: a round hall with a plank floor, a gallery on brackets, guild banners in green, red and brass, a great brass gear turning under the ceiling, clerks' desks, and a tall glowing board in a brass frame as the brightest thing in the room.

SHEET 8 — THE SHIP "WAYFARER". Side elevation, top view and front view of a small old sky ship, about 11 metres. A stubby wooden hull like a small 17th-century merchant ship, one mast with a big square wind-silk sail and a small triangular sail, two side "wind vanes" like fins, a glowing etherite crystal in a brass cage under the keel, a brass lantern at the stern, rope ladders, a gangway. Patched, loved, a little too small for its ambitions. Callouts for the crystal cage, vanes, lantern.

SHEET 9 — SPARK HARBOUR, establishing view. One wide painting-like view (still ink and watercolour, perspective allowed here): the floating island seen from slightly above at late afternoon, the windmill Exchange on its terrace, a ring of leaning half-timbered houses round a market square with awnings, a timber wharf and pier on stilts reaching out over the cloud sea with the ship moored, rope bridges to two tiny neighbouring islets, a thin waterfall falling off the cliff into the clouds, glowing crystals under the island, other islands in the hazy distance.

A page of small objects, each drawn alone with a label: street lamp post, market stall with awning, cargo crate, barrel, coil of rope, notice board, treasure chest, brass scales, ledger book, spyglass, anchor on a chain, mooring bollard, rope railing section, silk drying rack, signboards with pictograms (anchor, quill, coin, sail, loaf).

Start now with SHEET 6.
```
