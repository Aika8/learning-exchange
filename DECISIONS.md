# Decisions — increment 1 (order book)

Choices the brief left open. Each one is the simplest behaviour; revisit any of them deliberately.

- Engine ownership: per the intent, the agent writes `engine` and its tests; Aika reviews this file and tests behaviour via the REPL.
- Java version: the brief said 17, the intent said 25; Aika chose 25 (Gradle toolchain).
- Obsolete spec: `intent/01-order-book.spec.md` was already deleted in commit 38363bd; nothing to delete.
- Price and quantity are `long` integer ticks/units; no decimals, no tick size.
- Zero or negative quantity: rejected with `IllegalArgumentException` by `Order.limit` / `Order.market`.
- Zero or negative limit price: rejected with `IllegalArgumentException`.
- Market order has no price: `Order.market(side, qty)` takes none and `Order.Market` exposes none.
- `Order` is a sealed interface; the factories return the concrete records `Order.Limit` / `Order.Market`.
- No price bands or collars: a market order walks the whole opposite side.
- Self-trade: deferred until accounts/owners exist (increment 2).
- Time priority is arrival order into the level's deque; the caller-supplied `timestamp` was dropped with the old constructor, and the engine id reflects arrival order.
- Order ids are generated by `OrderBook` from a monotonic `long` counter starting at 1, per book instance; never reused, including after full fill or cancel.
- Every submitted order gets an id, including market orders and orders that fill completely and never rest.
- Id counter overflow is not handled.
- `submit` takes an `Order` (immutable parameters from the factories); submitting the same `Order` value twice creates two independent orders with two ids.
- `submit` returns `SubmitResult(orderId, trades)` with a derived `filledQty()`; it does not expose rested or cancelled quantity separately.
- The unfilled market remainder is computed by the caller as `order.qty() − result.filledQty()`.
- `Order` is immutable; remaining quantity of resting orders is tracked inside the book.
- `Trade` records buy order id, sell order id, price, qty and `aggressorSide` (the side of the incoming order, for limit and market orders alike).
- `cancel` returns false for unknown, already filled, or already cancelled ids.
- `cancel` removes from the level's deque in O(n) of that level; acceptable at learning scale.
- Read-only `bids(depth)` / `asks(depth)` returning aggregated `Level(price, qty, orders)` were added so the REPL can print the top 5 levels.
- Repl lives in package `repl`, outside `engine`.
- Repl prints the id assigned by the book after each submit; an order rejected by the factories never reaches the book and consumes no id.
- Repl: `book` prints only the book; unknown or malformed commands print an error and the book is not reprinted; end of input acts like `quit`.
- Repl prints asks highest-first above the spread and bids highest-first below it.

# Decisions — M1 (walking skeleton)

Engine and money
- `Exchange` wraps the untouched `OrderBook` and owns accounts (`Ledger`), order states and fees; owners live in `Exchange`, not in the book.
- One instrument `SILK-SPARK` (base SILK in whole units, quote SPARK in minor units, 2 decimals on screen). A price is SPARK minor units per 1 SILK.
- Fee: 10 bps of the notional on **each** side, in the quote asset, rounded **down** (so fees on parts never exceed the fee reserved for the whole). Both fees go to the account `exchange-fees`; money is moved, never destroyed — invariant 1 counts that account.
- Reservation: limit buy reserves `price × qty + fee`; market buy reserves the cost of walking the asks now + fee (exact, because the single writer sees the same book); any sell reserves `qty`. What a finished or cancelled order did not use is released.
- Market order into an empty opposite side is rejected (`NO_LIQUIDITY`) instead of accepted-and-expired; a market remainder after partial fill expires (`OrderExpired`).
- Self-trade is allowed (a bot may hit its own quote); fees are still charged. Prevention can come with M2 if it confuses statements.
- Exchange order ids are global (one counter), separate from the book's per-book ids; `Exchange` maps them.
- Rejections are not journalled (they change nothing) and come back as `Rejection` codes; REST maps them to 400/401/403/404/409/422 and a `Problem.code` that clients translate.
- Idempotency key is per account; it is stored in `OrderAccepted`, so it survives a restart.

Journal and replay (ADR-0005)
- The journal stores events, not commands. Replay re-executes the *decision* events (`AccountOpened`, `FundsDeposited`, `OrderAccepted`, `OrderCancelled`) and checks that the engine produces exactly the journalled *consequences* (`TradeExecuted`, `OrderExpired`); any difference stops the start loudly.
- `ClockChanged` is journalled; on start the simulation clock continues from the last journalled time with the last pause/speed.
- No snapshots yet: 32 000 events replay in well under a second. Snapshots when start-up gets slow.
- Journal I/O happens on the sequencer thread right after the command (buffered write + flush per command), not batched; simpler, fast enough for a learning load. Batching is the first optimisation if the sequencer becomes the bottleneck.
- File format: JSON lines with a `type` field (`{"type":"TradeExecuted",...}`), Jackson mix-in in the adapter so engine records carry no annotations. No version field yet; the first incompatible change adds `v` and an upcaster.
- `make reset` deletes `data/journal.jsonl`. With bots the journal grows ~30 MB per hour of running (the market maker re-quotes every step).

Concurrency (ADR-0006)
- One `Sequencer` (single platform thread) owns the whole `Exchange` (ledger + books) in M1. With one instrument that is "one writer per instrument"; per-instrument sequencers come with the second instrument, when cross-instrument reservation needs its own design.
- Race demonstration: `engine.OrderBookRaceTest` (8 threads on a bare `OrderBook` → corrupted state, asserted). Fix: `application.SequencerTest` runs the same workload through the sequencer and is always consistent.
- Pause freezes simulation time and stops the bots; people can still place and cancel orders while paused (orders get the frozen timestamp). Speed 1..60: the bot runner makes `speed` steps per real tick.

API and auth
- Base path `/api/v1`; instrument ids in paths are `SILK-SPARK` (no slash).
- Dev login: name → account id `acc-<lower-case name, spaces as dashes>`; token `dev-<accountId>`. Same name, any case → same account. Tokens survive restarts. Never for real money.
- A new account gets 100 000.00 SPARK and 1 000 SILK (`exchange.starting-balance.*`), journalled as deposits. `POST /me/deposits` is a dev top-up.
- `PUT /clock` needs any logged-in user (dev).
- One WebSocket `/ws`, JSON `{op, channel}`; snapshot on subscribe, then updates once per 100 ms tick; the account channel only nudges ("refetch"), it does not carry balances. Account channels need `?token=`.
- Money in JSON is `int64` numbers. TypeScript reads them as `number`, exact up to 2^53 — far above any practice balance; switch to strings if that ever changes.

Bots (ADR-0007)
- Bots run in-process and call the `TradingService` port only (ArchUnit forbids `bots` → `Exchange`/`OrderBook`/`ExchangeService`).
- A bot logs in like a person (gets the human starting balance) and, the first time only, deposits its `capital` from `bots.yaml`. "First time" = it has no orders yet.
- Fair value is a seeded random walk shared by the market maker; strategies get their own seeded `Random`. Same seed and same clock → same journal (`BotsTest`).
- Market maker cancels and re-quotes every step (simple, but it is most of the journal); inventory skew keeps it near `targetBase`.
- Only `market-maker` and `noise` exist in M1; the other strategies of ADR-0007 are M2.

Clients
- TypeScript 5.9 in the web client (openapi-typescript 7.13 requires `typescript ^5`); the mobile app keeps Expo's TypeScript 6. Mobile runs the generator with `npx openapi-typescript@7.13.0` to avoid the peer conflict.
- `openapi-typescript` (types only) + `openapi-fetch` (tiny runtime) instead of `orval`: no generated runtime code to read, typed paths and bodies.
- `money.ts` is copied into mobile; a test keeps both files identical until a shared package exists.
- Mobile tokens are a copy of `docs/design/tokens.json` (Metro does not read outside the app folder by default); a test keeps them equal.
- Expo Router ships stacks only, so the design's tab bar is a small own component on the main screens.
- Mobile shows the instrument as the API names it (`SILK/SPARK`); the real/lore catalogue (ADR-0012) maps names in M2. No game words otherwise.
- i18n tests: web — vitest (same keys, no empty values); mobile — `node --test` (same keys, no empty values, every `t('…')` key used in screens exists).
- Charts (ADR-0010) are not in M1; the instrument screen shows the last price and the book.

Game (Godot)
- Godot 4.7.2 stable, typed GDScript, GL Compatibility renderer; GUT 9.7.1 vendored in `game/addons/gut` (MIT) so tests run with no editor step.
- Scenes are a root node + script; the UI tree is built in code. Less `.tscn` noise to review, and widgets share `UI` helpers and one palette.
- i18n: one CSV (`game/i18n/strings.csv`: `keys,ru,en`) loaded at start into `TranslationServer` by the `Locale` autoload instead of Godot's CSV importer
  (`importer="keep"`): no generated `.translation` files, the same file is read by the test. Controls whose text is a key re-translate themselves; formatted texts listen to `Locale.locale_changed`.
- Godot's JSON parser returns floats; `BookModel` and the Ladder convert to `int` at the boundary (exact below 2^53, same limit as the TypeScript clients). Formatting and parsing of money never use floats (`Money`).
- M1 talks to the core API directly; `bff-game` (ADR-0004) and the catalogue of real/lore names (ADR-0012) are not there yet, so lore captions come from the game's own catalogue.
- The game logs in by captain's name with the same dev-login as the other clients; the same name = the same account in web and mobile.
- `-- --demo=<dir>` makes the game walk menu → harbour → Ladder, place a limit and a market order and save screenshots; it is the manual E2E for M1 (GUT covers logic, not the live socket).

# Decisions — M2a (stops, leverage, statement, deposits)

Scope: the first part of M2 — second instrument, conditional orders, leverage ×4 with stop-out, statement, deposits,
the matching client screens, CI. The catalogue/glossary, real market data, more bots, stocks with reports, bonds, ATR
and dividends are M2b/M2c.

Instruments
- `AMBER-SPARK` is the FX pair: AMBER is a foreign currency in whole units, priced in SPARK minor units (500.00 SPARK
  per AMBER at start), fee 5 bps (half the stock's: FX is more liquid). `Instrument.maxLeverage`: SILK 1, AMBER 4.
- `bots.yaml` now has a `markets:` list (the M1 single-market shape still parses). The FX dealer quotes ±0.30 SPARK
  around fair value — the spread a bank earns on an exchange office (vault: `finance/fx-market-kz.md`).

Engine, one writer (ADR-0008 accepted, ADR-0018 amends ADR-0006)
- Conditional orders, the margin monitor and deposits live in the engine, on the single writer, outside the books.
  After every command the queue of new trade prices is drained: each price, in order, goes to the conditional orders
  (by id) and then to the margin monitor (by position id). Generated orders use the same `placeInto` as a person's.
- Each print of a sweep is a "last trade price" in turn, so a stop can fire on the 2nd price of a 5-level sweep.
- A new conditional order waits for the next trade; it is not checked against the current last price.
- Nothing is reserved for a conditional order until it fires; the generated order may then be refused
  (`REJECTED` with the code, e.g. `INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`). That is how QUIK behaves too.
- A fired limit order with a price ≤ 0 (trailing TP with a big protective spread) is `REJECTED` / `INVALID_ORDER`.
- Conditional orders are on the person's own account only, not on a leveraged position's sub-account.
- Replay rule changed: after a decision, the journal must contain exactly the events the engine produces again, of any
  type; only an event that arrives when nothing is expected is a decision. Old M1 journals do not replay (new field `mid`
  on `TradeExecuted`): run `make reset`. No upcaster for a learning journal.

Leverage and stop-out
- A leveraged position gets a sub-account `owner~m<id>` (deposits: `owner~s<id>`; `~` is not allowed in names).
  The ledger shows exactly what it holds; reservation and fees work as for anyone.
- The bank (`exchange-bank`) is an ordinary account funded once at start by journalled deposits
  (`exchange.bank-capital.*`). It lends and pays interest, so invariant 1 holds without exceptions.
- Opening is at market only and only if the book is deep enough for the whole quantity (`NO_LIQUIDITY` otherwise).
  Long: collateral = ceil(notional / leverage) + fee, the bank lends the rest in SPARK. Short: collateral =
  ceil(proceeds / leverage), the bank lends the AMBER.
- Equity = quote held − quote loan + (base held − base loan) × last trade price. Stop-out when `2 × equity ≤ collateral`,
  checked on every trade price of that instrument — the same price stops use, never book quotes.
- Stop-out and close sell/buy at market. If the book is too thin to close, the position stays CLOSING and is retried
  on every following trade. If a short cannot afford to buy back all the AMBER, it buys what it can and the rest is
  bad debt. A gap through the stop-out can leave bad debt; the bank carries it (shown as `badDebt`).
- No interest on margin loans and no TOM/SPOT value dates in M2a.

Deposits
- One product: SPARK, 14% a year (`exchange.savings.rate-bps`), compounded daily, 365-day year. Each day adds
  `floor(balance × rate / 365)`. A bank day is `exchange.savings.day-millis` of simulation time — 60 000 (one sim minute)
  so compounding is visible in a demo (at ×60 a year passes in about 6 real minutes). The rate and day length are stored
  in `SavingsOpened`, so changing the config never changes an old deposit.
- A timer asks for accrual once a second (`AccrueInterest`, journalled only if interest was paid). A deposit whose
  interest rounds to zero keeps its last accrual time, so batching days gives the same money (tested).
- Closing pays out principal + interest accrued so far; the current partial day is lost.

Statement (projection, ADR-0005)
- Built from events only (`application.StatementProjection`), rebuilt from the journal on start, never read by the
  write side. Lines: deposits, trades, margin open/close, stop-out, deposit open/close, interest.
- Spread paid = (price − mid) × qty for a buy, (mid − price) × qty for a sell; mid = floor((best bid + best ask) / 2)
  just before the aggressive order arrived, stored on `TradeExecuted`. The resting side usually "pays" a negative spread
  (it earns it). No mid (one side empty) → 0.
- Realised profit: average-cost method per instrument, fees included in the cost and deducted from proceeds. A deposited
  base asset enters the cost basis at the last trade price, or at the first trade price if there was none yet.
  A leveraged position realises `returned − collateral` at close; its trades show with a `margin` flag and no profit.
- Tax: 10% of the period's realised profit (ИПН), never below zero, rounded down; losses in the period offset gains.
  Deposit interest is shown separately and not taxed (simplification).
- At most 20 000 lines are kept per person (bots trade all day); older lines drop out of the statement.

Build and CI
- `make test` installs client dependencies with `npm ci` (when `node_modules` is missing or older than the lock file),
  so it works in a clean clone. Without Godot on PATH the game tests are skipped with a message instead of failing.
- GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`): `make test` on Ubuntu with Java 25 and Node 24, on pushes to main and on
  pull requests. No Godot (no official headless image worth the setup yet) and no E2E (needs a running backend and
  browsers); both stay manual: `make test-game` / `make e2e` locally.
- `Idempotency-Key` stays on `POST /orders` only; the new POSTs (stops, positions, deposits) have none yet.
- Mobile has no leverage screen in M2a (web has it); mobile helpers in `src/finance.ts` are imported by `node --test`
  directly from TypeScript (Node ≥ 23.6 strips types).
- game3d harbour fps (ADR-0019): the Poly Haven tree carries ~700k leaf triangles drawn with a depth pre-pass and no
  LODs after load, which cost ~30 fps. `Assets.thinned()` keeps every 3rd leaf card, switches leaves to alpha scissor and
  regenerates LODs; plus LOD threshold 6 px and half-resolution GI. Worst harbour view: 41 → 65 fps (Apple M5, 1080p).
- game3d islands (ADR-0019): with assets the island mesh is `Proc.island_mesh_hd` (120 sides, 26 rings, noise cliffs
  with ledges, welded smooth normals; the cap stays flat inside 0.9 r so walking and props are unchanged), dressed with
  Poly Haven rock faces, cliffs, roots and pebbles; far islands get a light set. Thinned trees use `lod_bias` 2.5 so
  generated LODs don't strip the crown. Harbour stays ≥ 60 fps (worst view 64).
- game3d people (ADR-0019): villagers, Mara and hall traders are MakeHuman characters (CC0, ~30k tris, 53-joint game
  skeleton, static relaxed pose baked as rest pose, no animation yet), generated by `game3d/tools/people/build.sh`
  rather than locked downloads, because MakeHuman exports characters instead of hosting them. The captain stays
  procedural until walking animations exist. Harbour 58–66 fps on the worst views (Apple M5, 1080p).

## M2b
- Journal versioning (ADR-0020): every line ends with `,"v":2`; lines without it are v1 and are lifted by text-level
  upcaster steps (a JSON tree can't hold `OrderAccepted`, whose own `type` field repeats the event-type key).
  `JournalVersionsTest` replays a journal recorded by the M2a build. No more `make reset` after format changes.
- `Idempotency-Key` on `POST /conditional-orders`, `/margin/positions`, `/savings`, `/real-world/paper-positions`
  (one header component in the contract). Keys are scoped by account and by kind of command; journalled with the
  command so they survive replay. Paper keys live in the `PaperBook` projection (paper never touches the engine).
- Market "night" is per instrument: a stock is halted only on its report night (`TradingStatusChanged`), other
  markets keep trading. New orders, stops and margin opens are refused with `MARKET_CLOSED`; cancels are allowed.
- Gap model: a report moves the bots' fair value by surprise × 0.5 (10% EPS miss → −5%), clamped to ±30%; no
  post-earnings drift yet. The gap appears because market makers can't quote during the halt and quote around the new
  fair value at the open; noise traders' old limit orders can still fill at the old price.
- Dividends: record date = ex-date (T+0); holders at the ex-date morning are paid from the issuer's cash; fair value
  drops by the dividend. Coupons: one per season (91 bank days), paid from the issuer's cash, all or nothing.
- Default: when an issuer cannot pay the whole coupon, the service declares default; at maturity a defaulted bond is
  repaid with `floor(issuer cash / bonds held)`. GBND's issuer is funded for ~3.5 coupons on the bots' float, so it
  defaults on the 4th with almost nothing left — the "Щедрая гильдия" lesson.
- Tax: 10% on (realised profit + dividends + coupons) in the period, computed in the statement, not withheld.
- Deposit rate follows the policy rate: new deposits get policy − 200 bps (14% at the start rate of 16%); open
  deposits keep their rate.
- Yields and durations are computed in `double` (they are ratios, not money) and returned as integer bps / ×100;
  bond prices and every payment stay `long`.
- The calendar shows future rate decisions, reports, coupons and maturities (dates are public), never results,
  insider trades or storms ahead of time. A coupon's amount is a term of the bond, so it is shown in advance.
- Real-world data: `StaticSampleData` only (terms of live sources not checked); illustrative values, labelled
  "sample" on every screen. History endpoint takes `?symbol=` (a symbol like `USD/KZT` contains a slash).
- Catalogue and glossary are YAML files in `docs/lore/` read at start (repo-relative paths, like `bots.yaml`); the
  mobile BFF drops lore names, the game BFF keeps them. Tests run with the repo root as working directory.

## M2c
- ATR: Wilder, 14 daily candles (one bank day each), true range with the previous close, integer minor units rounded half
  up; `ready` only with 15 candles. A pure function over the candle projection, not journalled. ATR / last price is
  returned in bps for the helper. Position-size helper "stop = k × ATR": qty = floor(risk money / (k × ATR)); risk in R as before.
- Trade journal: `journal` on `POST /orders` (why ≤ 280 chars, market state and mood from fixed lists, planned risk =
  1 R in minor units). Journalled as `OrderAnnotated` right after the order; a retried Idempotency-Key does not
  annotate twice. Review result of an order = its fills against the last price, fees included (BUY: (last − avg) × qty,
  SELL: (avg − last) × qty). Not FIFO round trips: one rule for entries and exits, and a "sell" note is judged by
  what happened after it too. R × 100 = result × 100 / planned risk, rounded down.
- Dividends: ex-date = open of report day + 10, record = +1 day (T+1), pay = record + 4 days. Entitlement fixed at the
  ex-date open; reference price drops by the dividend until the next trade; resting orders are not adjusted.
  10% tax withheld at source per holder (rounded down) to `exchange-tax`; the statement shows DIVIDEND (gross) and
  DIVIDEND_TAX lines; `totals.tax` = 10% × (profit + payouts) − withheld, never below 0.
- Funds: IDX (2 000 SILK + 2 000 FORGE + 35 000.00 SPARK, TER 0.20%), ACT (600 SILK + 1 600 FORGE + 30 000.00 SPARK,
  TER 2%); 20 000 units each, held by bots (dealer 18 000). Fee = floor(NAV × TER × days / 365) from custody cash,
  charged by the interest timer for whole bank days; not charged while a holding has no price yet. Fund return =
  NAV change since the first fee day; basket return = (NAV + fees paid) change over the same time: the
  same shares and dividends without the fee (the custody receives SILK dividends). No rebalancing and no unit creation/redemption yet.
- WebSocketTest flake: `sendText().get()` only means the frame left the client; the orders could trade before the
  server registered `account.acc-ws-b`, so the account nudge went to nobody. The test now waits until every subscribe
  is answered (snapshot or error) before placing orders.

## M3 (game3d vertical slice)
- The day loop is `GameFlow` (ADR-0023): Port → Plan → Trading → Sailing → Port …, Chronicle only from Port. Guards: no
  plan without provisions (the emergency fund), no trading without Mara's approval, no sailing with cargo and no Anchor.
  Arriving moves Sailing → Port; Silk Reach's market is Trading on the other island (no second exchange).
- Mara's 1% is counted from the trading capital = SPARK − provisions (6 × 5 000.00), with both fees in the risk. The
  Anchor is a STOP_LIMIT sell whose limit is a quarter of the stop distance under the stop: a small slip still fills, a
  gap through it leaves the order out of the market — the Act III lesson. Mara suggests the stop 2 × ATR(14) under entry.
- Sailing sets the exchange clock to ×60 (PUT /clock, a dev endpoint) and ×1 on arrival: a voyage "takes days".
  The first voyage of a day always crosses a storm (a scripted lesson); the market's own regime is shown in its text.
- R per voyage = cash flow of that voyage's fills + open quantity × last price, over the planned risk; rounded toward
  zero. Expectancy = mean R over voyages that traded. Fills belong to the latest plan for the same instrument.
- Basket = IDX-SPARK (the index fund, M2c) — Бабушка Ветла's basket; the comparison applies its day's return to the
  whole trading capital.
- Statement lines are copied with a key (ts, order, side, price, qty, n-th identical line): one market order can fill
  several identical lines at one timestamp.
- «Рекорд, а акция упала»: a record is revenue *or* net profit above every earlier report. With seed 42 FORGE never has a
  record profit with a miss in 16 seasons, but season 15 has record revenue with an EPS miss (2.56 vs 2.83) — the real
  headline shape. Season 4 (EPS 1.11 vs 2.20) is the night the Anchor is gapped through.
- Lessons format their numbers through the chronicler's typed args (`m` amount, `d` signed result, `p` price, `r` R,
  `bps` rate, `dbps` signed change), so ru/en re-render on the fly.
- Walking: a procedural walk cycle on the MakeHuman game-engine rig (`Walker`), no downloaded animation. Quaternius
  animations would need retargeting in Blender and have no stable URLs.

## M4 (assistant and polish)
- Assistant facts are integers/codes with a kind (money, bps, R×100, count); the template formats them, the model gets
  them as `key=value (kind)` data lines inside `<facts>`. The game's SessionFacts reach the server as integers only.
- The advice guard is a list of regular expressions (ru/en), not a classifier: clear, testable, strict on purpose.
  False positives are cheap (the template answers). Java needs `UNICODE_CHARACTER_CLASS` for `\b` around Cyrillic.
- `assistant.use-key=false` disables Claude even when the env var is set (tests, demo).
- Achievements: seven, fixed in code (`AchievementsProjection.Id`); names are client i18n keys. Sub-accounts (margin)
  don't earn them. Rebuilt from the journal on start, so any journal version the upcasters read gives them.
- Book throttle: at most one snapshot per instrument per 250 ms, trailing edge kept, identical books (ignoring `ts`)
  not resent. Trades and account nudges are not throttled. No contract change: the channel still sends snapshots.
- Mobile: rebalancing check uses the simulation clock (182 bank days = half a year), so it can be seen in a demo; targets
  and the last check live in a persisted Zustand store (local, not server). Values are SPARK cash + holdings × last price.
- Lump sum vs DCA: every start in the last 180 daily closes, N equal parts on consecutive days; units counted in
  millionths to stay integer. Reports win share, average edge and the worst outcome of each — no single cherry-picked start.
- Mobile memo: the order-book row formatter is `useCallback`-stable so `memo` rows skip re-render; home and
  achievement rows are memoised.
- game3d fps: NOT fixed in M4. A `-s` SceneTree probe could not build the harbour (autoloads missing), so its numbers
  were of an empty sea; with that probe SDFGI showed 30–150 ms frames after far teleports, but that is not evidence for
  the real island. Next: a probe started as a normal run (`--fps-probe` flag in `Session`), then decide on SDFGI.

## G3D polish (docs/demo/G3D-polish.md)
- World audit as a tool, not a one-off: `WorldProbe` walks a 2 m grid with the captain's own capsule and `Mover`, and
  compares every collider with the triangles you see (a copy of each mesh on physics layer 20). Before 451 findings on
  1204 points, after 0 on 1270. The same probe on a 3 m grid is a GUT test, so a new hole fails the build.
- Colliders are made in one place (`Solid`) and bodies are never scaled: boxes for walls, convex solids for the mill,
  a hull per mesh for a downloaded model (transform baked into the points), 0.6 m prisms up a tree trunk. Plants (fern,
  grass, shrub, roots) stay walk-through on purpose.
- Islands and decks are fenced by invisible walls along `Archipelago.walk_areas` (the same outline the navmesh, the
  audit and the placement use: `Proc.cap_radius`, `Proc.cap_height`). Decks also have visible rope rails. A captain who

## G3D techniques (ADR-0029)

  coloured, bright, not green: warm cloth is rust, cool cloth middle blue. Patterns only on cloth (cell 1.1 m),
  roofs (1.5 m) and the cloud sea (14 m); they lighten the tone by 55 % towards cream; faded out below 14–30 px a cell.

  50–95 % by brightness and by half again with distance; the line is moved up to 1.6 px by slow noise.

## G3D drawn comic panels (amendment to ADR-0037)
- Aika's decision (2026-11-18): an image model draws the comic panels **without any text**; balloons, captions and
  name plates stay the game's, over the picture, so translations and rewrites need no redrawing.
- `docs/design/comic-script.md` — per talk, page and panel: the place, who is in frame, mood, shot, panel shape and
  size, where to leave room for the balloons, the sheets to attach, and a ready English prompt. It is generated by
  `game3d/tools/comic_script.py` from `talks.json`; run it again after talks change. One style for every island
  (sheet 06), the island's own technique only as a hint for the background.
- A picture is `game3d/art/comic/<talk>/<page>-<panel>.jpg` (or `.png`), counted from 1. `ComicPage.art()` loads it
  from the file (`art/` is not imported) and caches it, null when there is none; the frame then draws it cut to
  itself from the middle (cover) instead of the softened scene and the cut-out figures, with no marks round heads.
- `"tail": {"<who>": [x, y]}` on a panel — where that one's mouth is, as shares of the frame — overrides the mouth
  the tails point at, in drawn panels and in put-together ones. Without it, a drawn panel's mouths are at 27 % (one
  person) or 25 % / 75 % (two) across and 42 % down: where the prompts place the people.
- `make check-game3d-data` checks that every picture names an existing talk, page and panel, and every tail a person
  in that panel with both shares in 0…1.
